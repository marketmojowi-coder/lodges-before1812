Background reading for the Potomac Territory

  • Amos B. Casselman, “The Virginia Portion of the District of Columbia,” Records of the Columbia Historical Society, Washington, D.C. 12 (1909): 115–141.
    The choice of the federal district on both banks of the Potomac, Alexandria County's place in it from 1801, and its return to Virginia in 1846. Held by: Historical Society of Washington, D.C.; JSTOR, stable URL jstor.org/stable/40066996. Published 1909; public domain in the United States. The digitized copy is used under JSTOR's terms.
  • Donald M. Robey, “Alexandria Lodge No. 39, Alexandria, Virginia, 1783–1788,” paper read at a stated meeting of Alexandria-Washington Lodge No. 22, September 23, 1999.
    The lodge's Pennsylvania warrant as No. 39 (February 3, 1783), its move to the Grand Lodge of Virginia as No. 22 (April 28, 1788, with George Washington as Master), and its members, drawing on Franklin L. Brockett, The Lodge of Washington. Held by: Alexandria-Washington Lodge No. 22, A.F. & A.M., Alexandria, Virginia. An unpublished paper; summarized with credit, not quoted at length. Ask the lodge before reproducing it.

Method

  • Primary records first. Originals outrank later histories; where they disagree, the original is followed and the difference is noted.
  • Counted by document. Statistics count original documents, not repeated mentions.
  • Names from the lodge rolls. The rolls are the authority for spelling; other spellings are kept as variants.
  • Marked by kind. Every record says whether it is an original, a line compiled in a book, or a note from the author's research workbook, which is never counted as primary.
  • Private records excluded. The minute books and ledger of Federal Lodge No. 15 and the Royal Arch minutes, which belong to the lodge, are not included. The Commissioners' records program is published separately.
